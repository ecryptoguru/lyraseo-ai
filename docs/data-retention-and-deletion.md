# LyraSEO AI: approved data retention and deletion policy

Updated: 2026-10-22. Status: founder-approved product/engineering defaults. This is not an implemented privacy policy, verified processor configuration or authorization to delete existing data. Azure OpenAI is the selected LLM processor; account-specific settings and statutory coverage remain verification gates. Approved phase and budget decisions remain unchanged.

## 1. Authority and retention principles

This document owns the approved retention durations, deletion triggers and backup boundaries, superseding earlier blanket proposals of twelve months for all results/briefs/reports and thirty days for all backups. The founder approved these defaults on 2026-10-22; they do not need repeat approval. [Release phases](./release-phases.md) continues to own timing; privacy cleanup, security records and deletion processing are phase-one operational work even though customer automation and dedicated PostHog monitoring are deferred.

Keep compact, attributable evidence longer than bulky source captures. Keep deliberately saved customer work until its owner deletes it or the workspace lifecycle ends. Avoid indefinite retention of raw inputs, duplicate provider payloads and debug bodies. A page being public does not make its personal data non-sensitive. A hash, pseudonymous actor ID or redacted URL is not automatically anonymous.

Durations run from capture, import or event creation unless a row says otherwise; viewing, downloading, retrying delivery or copying a cache entry must not extend expiry. Use UTC timestamps and calendar-month arithmetic for month-based windows, displaying expiry in the user's timezone. User-requested deletion overrides ordinary product TTLs, subject only to specifically documented legal/security records. A shorter provider license/storage limit wins. Store an expiry and data-category tag for artifacts and rows, and expose meaningful expiry information in the UI.

## 2. Approved default schedule

| Data category | Approved retention | Quality/privacy boundary |
| ------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------ |
| Anonymous preview raw HTML/DOM | Processing only; no persistent raw archive | Crash/temporary files cleaned within 24 hours; no bodies in logs |
| Normalized anonymous preview | 24 hours from completion | Private result capability, no indexing; expiry also applies to cached results |
| Full-audit HTML, rendered DOM, full extracted source text and acquired screenshots if needed | 30 days from capture | Private compressed artifacts; no unsolicited screenshots; keep source excerpts only where needed for findings |
| Research extraction/source snapshots | 30 days from acquisition; workspace research reuse cache 24 hours | Cache freshness is distinct from evidence retention; selected citations/minimal excerpts may remain with saved work |
| Raw AI observation answers and full provider response envelopes | 90 days from observation, where provider terms allow | Keep minimal mention/citation evidence and configuration with the longer-lived result; do not keep duplicate full answers under another label |
| Audit findings, scores/coverage, observation metrics/citation excerpts and GSC imports/metrics | Rolling 13 months from run/import | Enables annual comparisons with overlap; preserve versions, cohort, timestamps and incompleteness; bounded GSC imports are not a complete search index |
| Saved briefs, drafts, revisions explicitly pinned by a user, saved prompt panels and deliberately saved final reports | While the workspace is active, until deleted | Do not silently expire authored work at 13 months; respect plan storage limits transparently; unpinned historical/autosave revisions expire after 90 days |
| Generated PDF/CSV download cache | Seven days from generation | Reuse retained results and report configuration for export; no new crawl/model call just to download; a deliberately saved final report follows the saved-work rule |
| Raw webhook/request debug payloads | Do not persist by default; exceptional redacted failure payloads up to seven days | Preserve normalized event ID, signature-verification outcome, state transition and retry/settlement receipts separately; no secret headers/card data |
| Routine diagnostic detail outside required records | Up to seven days, or shorter native provider window | Not a promise of seven days on every plan; no raw content, tokens or prompt/answer logging; never use this TTL to erase legally required ICT records |
| Required ICT/security/admin-action archive | Rolling 180 days, with required records maintained in India | Restricted records, necessary fields only; applicability, coverage and any longer mandatory rule must be validated before launch; see section 6 |
| Ordinary support/recovery correspondence | 90 days after case closure | Keep only a minimal decision/action receipt in the security archive; no identity-document collection by default; disputes/holds scoped separately |
| LyraSEO AI email delivery receipts | 30 days from delivery/failure | Message ID, purpose, status and safe actor references; no OTP or rendered body; Brevo's separate minimum applies below |
| Credentials and active auth records | Only while needed; revoke on disconnect/deletion | OTP ten-minute validity and existing session policy are unchanged; expiry is not permission to keep plaintext secrets afterward |
| Operator-managed backups | Daily, seven-day rolling restore window | Encrypted and access-separated; provider-held copies have their own verified contract; details in section 4 |
| Billing/accounting records | Applicable statutory minimum and specifically documented dispute holds | Retain necessary invoice/payment/ledger facts only, separately from page/content/GSC data; legal-entity-specific schedule required before live billing |

The 13-month result window does not secretly preserve full source text or full AI answers. Old results show which evidence has expired. Exact historical re-evaluation or a new rewrite requiring deleted source input needs explicit reacquisition and any normal allowance; never claim a fresh fetch reproduces the old snapshot. A saved document may retain citations or an explicitly saved answer excerpt: treat it as an intentional saved copy, disclose its lifecycle and include it in deletion, rather than silently copying all raw material into saved work.

Use R2 Standard initially for compressed bulky artifacts, indexed metadata and bounded queryable facts in PostgreSQL, and exact deduplication only within the same workspace. Preserve immutable provenance and versions; do not share private artifacts across tenants to save storage. Avoid automatic cold-tier transitions for seven/thirty-day objects or adding a warehouse/vector index for retention. R2 Standard has a published free allowance, while Infrequent Access adds retrieval/operation tradeoffs; this is an optimization default, not a verified account entitlement or a reason to change evidence quality.

## 3. Deletion, revocation and workspace lifecycle

Approved deletion default: after an authenticated, authorized confirmation, block new product access/processing for the deleted scope immediately and complete LyraSEO AI-controlled active-store cleanup within seven days. This is a target to verify before publication, not a claim of current capability or instant erasure from every processor. No default thirty-day undo/trash period for an explicit deletion request. Offer export before the irreversible confirmation; do not delay a confirmed deletion to force an export.

Use the approved recent-authentication/MFA policy for sensitive deletion. Enforce owner/site permissions and show the exact scope. A member deleting their personal account does not delete another owner's workspace. A last owner must transfer ownership or explicitly delete the workspace; process lawful identity-erasure requests without leaving them stuck indefinitely behind an ownership UI. Remove personal profile/provider identities and minimize attribution in retained shared work, while preserving justified workspace-owned records and clearly identifying the limits of any pseudonymization.

For site/workspace deletion, persist a deletion marker and outbox work in the same authoritative transaction. Revoke scoped grants, product API tokens, result capabilities and integration credentials; stop new job admission and phase-two schedules. Account deletion also revokes the user's auth sessions, while a site/workspace deletion removes access to that scope through current authorization checks. Existing jobs, callbacks, queues and retries must check the marker before saving results or sending data, and must not recreate deleted rows/objects. Cancel accepted provider tasks when supported; still reconcile unavoidable provider charges without restoring product access. For deleted workspaces with subscriptions, request and verify cancellation of future renewals through the selected billing contract before declaring the billing cleanup complete. Preserve the minimum identifiers/settlement facts needed for cancellation, owed transactions and lawful records.

Inventory and purge scoped PostgreSQL product/auth data, R2 objects, download caches, research/observation snapshots, GSC imports, saved content, invitation artifacts and configured downstream processor records. Request supported processor erasure promptly and record its receipt/status. Delete auth users through supported provider APIs rather than mutating managed Supabase tables. Better Auth identity/session/credential deletion follows its reviewed Prisma contract. Preserve a small opaque deletion receipt/marker for 180 days or longer only if a still-restorable approved backup requires it; no copied customer content. A pending/failed processor purge must remain visible as such, not be marked globally complete.

Default on integration disconnect: stop new imports/deliveries immediately, revoke/remove credentials, and purge that integration's imported dataset within seven days. Explain this before confirmation and allow export beforehand. Keep independently authored workspace documents under the saved-work rule, with removed source references visibly unavailable; do not retain full imported datasets disguised inside derived artifacts. A separate verified retention choice would need explicit review rather than assuming a disconnected OAuth grant permits ongoing processing.

For private test access ending or a paid service term ending without renewal, use the approved 30-day read-only export window with no new expensive work, followed by the seven-day cleanup process. Notify at the end of access and before purge. This approves data availability only, not a new refund or payment-failure grace policy. The approved auth cutover preserves private-test accounts and retained product data, with fresh sign-in on Better Auth. Do not reset data or restart its retention clock during migration; apply existing expiry/deletion policies and verified identity mapping. Never erase an active paid workspace solely for login inactivity. Free/public-account inactivity rules and public billing grace/refund terms remain commercial decisions; no new automatic dormant-account purge is selected here.

Use private downloads with a maximum five-minute signed-URL lifetime and no shared cache for private artifacts. Revoking application access cannot by itself revoke every already-issued direct URL; delete the underlying object promptly where required or use an authorized download handler. Describe any remaining bounded URL window accurately. Already downloaded files, delivered customer webhooks and WordPress drafts live in customer-controlled systems; LyraSEO AI deletion does not silently erase those external copies or publish/delete CMS content on the user's behalf.

## 4. Backups and preventing resurrection

Supabase currently documents daily Pro backups with a seven-day accessible restore window. Free projects need an explicit export/backup process rather than an assumed equivalent service. Approved operator policy: daily encrypted logical backups to a separate private, access-restricted backup location, keeping seven daily restore points, plus native Pro backups when production uses Pro. Back up non-reproducible saved customer artifacts and their manifest separately; a database backup is not an R2 object backup. Do not duplicate every temporary HTML capture or export cache as a long-lived archive. Restore critical customer work before declaring the backup design adequate.

A seven-day rolling backup TTL is measured from backup creation. If active-store deletion takes seven days, an operator-managed copy taken just before that purge can survive another seven days: up to fourteen days from confirmed deletion. Do not state that backup erasure occurs seven days from the initial request. Provider physical-copy erasure is governed by its contract and is not established by the dashboard's accessible restore window. Financial/security archives intentionally retained under their own rules are not ordinary deleted product data.

Backups are isolated from product reads. A restore must replay deletion markers and current TTLs before user access, deliveries, schedules or jobs resume, and must not revive revoked credentials or old sessions. Keep deletion receipts outside the same restorable snapshot so the restore cannot erase the evidence needed to reapply them. Test both relational and artifact deletion after restore. Do not authorize project-wide destruction to satisfy one user's deletion.

Daily backup cadence permits loss of up to a day's changes; do not promise zero-loss recovery. PITR or stronger journal/backup arrangements are a separate measured production-budget decision if required for paid billing/usage recovery. No paid PITR purchase or claim that all accounting can safely tolerate that loss is approved by this retention policy. Demonstrate reconciliation/recovery before customer launch. Backup egress, storage and any artifact copies are infrastructure costs outside the $10 per user AI/research/measurement envelope.

### 4.1 Approved pilot recovery objective

Founder-approved private-pilot recovery objective (2026-10-22): restore usable service within 24 hours of a confirmed incident, using the approved daily encrypted backups and seven-day rolling history. With successful daily backups, up to 24 hours of recent test changes may be lost. These are internal pilot objectives to verify in a restore exercise, not a public availability guarantee or proof that free-tier recovery is already configured. Confirm production recovery and billing/usage reconciliation requirements in phase 2 before live customer launch.

The founder is the approved initial human operator; engineering prepares and verifies the restore procedure. Restore-time deletion replay, revocation and reconciliation remain required under the existing policy.

## 6. Required records and India-specific review

CERT-In's published directions require covered service providers/intermediaries/data centres/body corporates and government organisations to enable ICT-system logs and maintain them for a rolling 180 days within Indian jurisdiction. The approved engineering baseline is a minimal, restricted 180-day required-log archive maintained in India, distinct from seven-day diagnostic detail and PostHog EU. This must cover applicable systems/fields, not just selected admin events. Validate applicability, any longer current obligation and collection/export coverage before real-data private testing or launch as applicable; do not infer compliance from pseudonymous event names alone.

Use the Mumbai primary database for a small pilot's restricted required-record archive if technically appropriate and sufficient; the R2 APAC location hint is not an India-residency guarantee for that archive. Reassess storage/partition/export needs at measured scale. Keep full page text, AI prompts/answers, OTPs and tokens out of these records. Retain only justified identifiers/network fields where required for the reviewed security purpose, with restricted access, expiry and an attributable access trail. Necessary security logging is not permission to collect a product-analytics backlog or activate phase-two monitoring.

Billing records require a separate entity-specific statutory schedule before live billing. For example, CGST Act section 36 ties relevant record retention to 72 months from the annual-return due date, with longer retention in specified proceedings; company/accounting obligations may differ. Do not replace this with a guessed universal seven-year TTL or retain customer page/content data merely because an invoice must remain. Record category, legal basis, retention end, hold reason and reviewer; release holds and delete when the requirement ends. Only minimal retained records survive a normal product deletion, and the deletion confirmation must describe the exception honestly.

## 7. Enforcement, verification and approval

Expiry is an access rule as well as storage cleanup. Check `expires_at`/deletion state before reads, exports and new generation. Reuse the planned operational worker/outbox for paginated, idempotent cleanup and a daily reconciliation of missed objects; this is phase-one maintenance, not deferred customer automation. R2 lifecycle rules provide a backstop, but the provider documents typical deletion lag and does not guarantee an exact instant. Private artifacts need an authoritative manifest that deletion can enumerate without cross-tenant leakage. Keep deduplication references workspace-scoped and remove a shared-within-workspace object only when no retained authorized reference needs it.

Before publication verify expiry-boundary access, early user deletion, member-versus-workspace scope, last-owner handling, in-flight callbacks, duplicate cleanup, credential revocation, exported/pinned copies, retained financial/security exceptions, processor failure tracking and deletion after restore. Keep policy version and safe completion receipts. Test the Supabase private-phase path and the Better Auth production path independently. No account data, projects, buckets, logs, DNS or provider settings were inspected or changed during planning.

The founder approved the retention schedule and deletion defaults on 2026-10-22. Account-specific processor settings, the actual legal-entity statutory schedule, required-log coverage and production recovery evidence remain verification gates before customer launch; they are not presented as already satisfied. Recording approval does not execute deletion or configure providers.
